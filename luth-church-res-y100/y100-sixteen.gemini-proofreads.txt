  2. Potentially Unclear to the Reader
  
  • The merger year 1921 isn't stated in the body narrative (Pages 6–7, y100-sixteen.typ:166-189):
  1921-2026 appears on the front cover, and Page 6 says *"Almost forty years after they had divided [1883], the two churches realized that it was time to get together 
  again... They agreed to reunite..."* followed immediately by a photo captioned 1924, and then Page 7 says *"After five years of fund-raising... dedicated on December
  5, 1926."* Without 1921 named in the text, a reader can be momentarily thrown by the 1924 photo before doing the 1926 − 5 math.
  • "the Church House rooms" (Page 11, y100-sixteen.typ:272-273):
  Page 10 introduces the former Woman’s Club building as "our Church House" (with the carriage house behind it becoming the Recreation Hall). Page 11 then says "The   
  temporary classrooms behind the church and the Church House rooms were no longer needed, so they were torn down." A reader unfamiliar with the campus might wonder   
  whether the entire Church House building was demolished (leaving only the carriage house behind it) or just certain rooms/additions.
  • Uncaptioned photo on Page 12 (y100-sixteen.typ:295-297):
  Unlike most of the other photos, renovation-1997.JPG has no caption or embedded label. Visitors may not know whether the white-gabled entrance shown there is part of
  the 1997 work that still stands or the Old Reiser Hall replaced in 2019 on the facing page.
  • "Old Reiser Hall, built in the 1800s" (Page 13, y100-sixteen.typ:305):
  Readers reading closely from Page 10 (former carriage house → Recreation Hall) through Page 12 (renamed Reiser Hall in 1997) can piece this together, though someone 
  skimming the bold timeline dates may briefly wonder how a hall at a 1926 church—named Reiser Hall in 1997—was built in the 1800s.

  3. Factual, Quotation & Licensing Checks
  
  All key dates, names, addresses, and anniversary counts check out (Sunday, December 6, 2026 and December 5, 1926 are both Sundays; 8 steps in the building list;     
  Pastor Peter Meister in Feb 1859; 548 Walker St.; 1883 split & 557 Greene St. / Metropolitan Community Church; 1910 histories by Pastors Finck and Derrick; Scroggs &
  Ewing; 1996 75th Diamond Jubilee; Walter Allen & Cora Shealy Reiser; Pastor David B. Hunter; Feb 2020 Reiser Hall dedication & Historic Augusta New Construction     
  Award; Hebrews 12:1; 825 Greene St. & (706) 724-8792). A few items worth checking:
  
  • Hymn wording, attribution, and CC BY 4.0 (Page 2, y100-sixteen.typ:60-76, and Page 16, y100-sixteen.typ:360-368):
      • The lines on Page 2 are from "We Are the Church" by Richard K. Avery and Donald S. Marsh (© 1972 Hope Publishing Co.).
      • Wording: The published hymn starts each of the four verse lines with "The" rather than "A" ("The church is not a building..."), and the refrain has two lines  
      in the middle ("We are the church together! / All who follow Jesus, all around the world!") before "Yes, we’re the church together."
      • Licensing: Because a 1972 hymn is still under copyright (usually reprinted by churches under OneLicense or CCLI with a copyright line), you may want to credit 
      Avery & Marsh / Hope Publishing and make sure the back cover’s CC BY 4.0 license clearly applies to your mother’s text rather than the quoted hymn lyrics.       
  • "These buildings" at 548 Walker Street (Page 4, y100-sixteen.typ:124-125):
  The text mentions the church and the added Schoolhouse, then says "(These buildings are now used by the Chinese Benevolent Association.)" Your mother likely knows   
  the property firsthand, so just double-check that the schoolhouse structure still stands and is used alongside the 1860 brick church building (and note that the     
  group’s full name is the Chinese Consolidated Benevolent Association, though shortening it is completely natural).
  • 1910 "Golden Jubilee" math nuance (Page 6, y100-sixteen.typ:162-163):
  Page 4 gives February 1859 as the start of Lutheran worship in Augusta, while Page 6 says "In 1910 the two churches celebrated a 'Golden Jubilee' — 50 years of      
  Lutheran worship in Augusta." Historically, St. Matthew’s celebrated its Golden Jubilee in January 1910—50 years after dedicating its 1860 church building (and ~51  
  years after worship began in 1859). This is completely fine in context, just a one-year arithmetic detail an attentive reader might notice.
